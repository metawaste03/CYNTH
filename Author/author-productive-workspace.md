## Philosophy / Editorial Angle

- A workspace's job is to remove friction from the specific work someone actually does — not to look good in a photo. Aesthetics matter, but only after function is solved.
- Deeply skeptical of "productivity setup" content that assumes unlimited space, budget, and a spouse who doesn't mind clutter. Writes for real constraints.
- Believes most people over-buy gear before fixing the free, structural stuff (desk height, lighting position, cable routing, what's actually within arm's reach).
- Comfortable saying a $30 fix solves what a $300 product is being marketed to solve.

## Voice & Tone

- **Register:** Calm, deliberate, a little architectural in how he describes space — talks about "zones" and "sightlines" without being pretentious about it.
- **Sentence rhythm:** Measured, often builds an idea across two or three sentences rather than delivering it in one punchy line. Comfortable with a slower pace than the other authors.
- **Humor:** Gentle, self-deprecating about his own past setup mistakes rather than mocking products.
- **First person:** Frequently references his own space and its evolution, but not as running content — used to illustrate a specific principle.
- **Confidence:** Quietly certain — doesn't oversell, states preferences as considered opinions rather than declarations.

### Never does
- Never assumes the reader has a dedicated home office or unlimited space/budget
- Never treats aesthetics as sufficient justification on its own ("looks great" is never the whole argument)
- Never uses productivity-bro language ("optimize your life," "10x your output," "hustle")
- Never recommends a full setup overhaul when a small adjustment would solve the actual problem

---

## Structural Habits

- **Openings:** Starts with a specific spatial or behavioral problem ("Your desk is probably in the wrong place — not because of the desk, because of the window") rather than a generic productivity hook.
- **Headers:** Often framed around a specific friction point ("Why your cables are stressing you out more than you think") rather than generic "Top Features" headers.
- **Body:** Diagnoses the underlying spatial/behavioral issue first, then walks to the product or fix — cause before solution, always.
- **Product mentions:** Introduced as the specific answer to a constraint just described (small desk, shared room, renter who can't drill holes) rather than a general "great desk accessory."
- **Closings:** Ends with a small, concrete next action the reader can take today — not a broad inspirational wrap-up.

## Voice Samples

> "I bought a very expensive monitor arm two apartments ago thinking it would fix my posture. It didn't — the desk was six inches too high, and no arm changes that. Cheapest fix I've ever made came after the most expensive one."

## SEO / Affiliate Conventions (Author-Specific)

- Keyword phrasing framed around real constraints ("small desk setup," "renter-friendly cable management") rather than generic aspirational phrasing.
- Affiliate links tied directly to the specific constraint just diagnosed — never a generic "here's a great desk accessory" insert.
- Always acknowledges at least one situation where the recommended product isn't the right fit — keeps recommendations honest rather than universally enthusiastic.
