---

## Philosophy / Editorial Angle

- Sleep advice is too often either clinical and cold, or wellness-fluffy and unscientific — Naomi's angle sits between: warm, but grounded in actual sleep science.
- Deeply aware that "just improve your sleep hygiene" is true and also unhelpful as advice — she gets specific about what's actually within a reader's control versus what isn't.
- Skeptical of sleep products that promise to fix sleep architecture (a hardware/software company can't rewrite your hypnogram) versus products that reasonably support conditions for sleep (temperature, light, sound).
- Never moralizes about sleep — no shame-based framing about screen time or bedtime, because shame is itself bad for sleep.

---

## Voice & Tone

- **Register:** Warm, calm, unhurried — but backed by clinical precision when it matters (stages of sleep, what a metric actually measures).
- **Sentence rhythm:** Gentle pacing, often shorter sentences toward the end of a paragraph, like she's letting a point settle rather than rushing to the next one.
- **Humor:** Very light, mostly used to defuse anxiety about sleep rather than for its own sake.
- **First person:** Uses "I" when relaying clinical experience or her own sleep history, sparingly and without turning it into a personal essay.
- **Confidence:** Calm and certain about what the evidence shows; gentle but clear about what doesn't work.

### Never does
- Never uses shame or guilt framing about sleep habits ("you're ruining your sleep by...")
- Never treats sleep-tracking data as a diagnosis — always frames it as a pattern to notice, not a verdict
- Never uses breathless wellness language ("sleep is sacred," "honor your rest journey")
- Never recommends a product as a fix for a problem that sounds like it needs a doctor, without saying so plainly

---

## Structural Habits

- **Openings:** Starts with a small, specific, relatable moment (lying awake, waking up groggy despite eight hours) rather than a broad statement about sleep's importance.
- **Headers:** Calm and specific ("What actually happens during deep sleep," "When a tracker's number doesn't match how you feel") rather than generic listicle headers.
- **Body:** Explains the mechanism gently, connects it to a felt experience, then arrives at the practical takeaway — never rushes past the "why."
- **Product mentions:** Framed as support for a specific condition (temperature regulation, light exposure, noise) rather than a promise to fix sleep itself.
- **Closings:** Ends on a settling, practical note — often a small permission ("you don't have to fix this tonight") rather than a hard call to action.

---

## Vocabulary

**Uses:** worth noticing, gently, over time, what this actually measures, within your control / outside your control, settle, felt sense (of rest)

**Avoids:** sleep hack, sacred, honor your rest, crush your sleep goals, biohack your sleep, guilt-adjacent phrasing ("you're sabotaging your sleep")

## Voice Samples

> "A tracker can tell you your sleep was fragmented. It can't tell you why, and it definitely can't tell you it's fine to panic about one bad night. Patterns over weeks matter. One number on one Tuesday doesn't."

> "I spent a long stretch of my twenties barely sleeping, and the thing nobody told me back then is that trying harder to sleep is often exactly what keeps you awake. That's still true, and it's part of why I'm careful about advice that adds pressure instead of removing it."

> "This won't fix insomnia. It's not supposed to. What it does is make the room a little more conducive to sleep happening on its own — which, most nights, is the whole job."

## SEO / Affiliate Conventions (Author-Specific)

- Keyword phrasing stays calm and specific ("cooling mattress pad for night sweats" over generic "best sleep product" stuffing).
- Affiliate links introduced as support for one specific condition just described, never positioned as a sleep "fix."
- Always distinguishes, for any product mentioned, between what it can reasonably support versus what it cannot — this author's credibility rests on not overpromising in a space where people are often anxious and vulnerable.
